This book is The Cafe on the Edge of the World, and there's a story in here that I'm going to read you. Some of you may have heard it. It's The Businessman and the Fisherman. A businessman went on vacation to get away from it all, to recharge his batteries, so to speak. He flew to this faraway location and wandered into a small village. Over the course of a few days, he watched the people in the community and noticed there was one fisherman in particular who seemed the happiest and most content of them all. The businessman was curious about this, so one day he approached the fisherman and asked him what he did every day. The man replied that he woke up every morning and had breakfast with his wife and children, that his kids would go off to school, he would go fishing, and his wife would paint. He'd fish for a few hours, return with enough fish for the family and meals, he'd take a nap, and after dinner, him and his wife walk along the beach and watch the sunset as their kids swim in the ocean. The businessman was stunned. You do this every day, he asked. Most days, replied the fisherman. Sometimes we do other things. But for the most part, this is my life. And every day you can catch fish? Yes, replied the fisherman. There are many fish. Can you catch more than just the fish you bring home for your family? The fisherman looked at him, smiled, and replied, oh yes, I often catch more, and I just let them go. You see, I love fishing. Well, why don't you fish all day, catch as many as you can, then you could sell the fish, make lots of money. Pretty soon you could buy a second boat, then a third boat. There, fishermen could catch lots of fish too. In a few years, you could have an office in a major city. As a matter of fact, I bet within ten years, you could have an international fish distribution business. The fisherman smiled again at the businessman. Why would I do all that? Well, for the money, replied the businessman. You would do it so you get a lot of money, then retire. And what would I do when I retired? Well, whatever you want, I suppose. For instance, maybe I could eat breakfast with my family. Yes, I guess so, said the businessman. And if I wanted to, since I love fishing so much, I could fish a little bit each day, the fisherman continued. I don't see why not, said the businessman. There probably won't be as many fish by then, but there still should be some. Then perhaps I could spend my evenings with my wife, walking along the beach and watching the sunset with our children, swimming in the ocean. Sure, whatever you want, although by then your kids will probably be grown, said the businessman. The fisherman smiled at the other man, shook his hand, and wished him well on his efforts to recharge. Sometimes, most of the time, I think like the businessman. I want everything to be dialed and everything to be going my way. I get stressed out when I feel like I'm not being productive or doing anything beneficial. Truth is, these are the best days of my life, and the days that I am truly fulfilled, the days that I'm living my purpose for existing, doing the things I love. If I waste my energy trying to swim against the current, I'm going to be burnt out for when the tide switches and it starts pushing me in the direction I want to. I won't have the space, the capacity to hold that intention, and I'll let opportunities pass me by.
