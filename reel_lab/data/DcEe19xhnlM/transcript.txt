Five stoic, all-time one-liners. The first, from Seneca, he says, he who suffers before it is necessary suffers more than is necessary. Don't torture yourself with anxiety. Don't torture yourself with catastrophizing. Don't torture yourself by all the bad things that can happen. Stay in the moment. Two, from Marcus Aurelius, the impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way. This is the famous idea that the obstacle is not in the path, it is the path. It's giving you a chance to do things, to do things differently, to do things in a way that you wouldn't have chosen, but in a way ultimately that makes you better for having to wrestle with it. Number three from Epictetus, it's impossible to learn that which you think you already know. Ego is the enemy. It prevents you from learning more. It prevents you from hearing what you need to hear. It prevents you from seeing what you need to see. Number four, for Marcus Aurelius, the happiness of your life depends on the quality of your thoughts. He says basically that our life is dyed by what we think about. If we choose to see everything as awful and sad and gloomy and impossible, well, it'll look that way, which goes to the idea that the obstacle is the way. How are you going to see this? Do you see it as an opportunity or as an obstacle? And number five, finally from Epictetus, it's not what happens to you, it's how you react that matters. It's about what you do with it. It's about what you make of it. It's about who you become because of it.
