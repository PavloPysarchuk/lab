As a kid who's always despised reading, there was one story I read in sophomore English class that changed the texture of my life, and I'm going to read it to you. It's called How Much Land Does a Man Need? A peasant named Pahom overhears his wife argue that a peasant life, though hard, is safe and free. He thinks, if I had enough land, I wouldn't fear the devil himself. The devil listening from behind the stove smiles. We'll see about that. Pahom gets his first plot of land. It feels like paradise, but neighbors trespass, relationships sour. He hears of better land elsewhere, more of it, cheaper. He sells everything and moves. The new land is good, but soon, not enough. He hears of even better land further east, Bashkir territory, practically free. He travels hundreds of miles. The Bashkir chief makes him an offer. Walk as much land as you can circle in one day. Sunrise to sunset. And it's yours. All just for a small price of a thousand rubles. One condition. You must return to the starting point before the sun goes down or you lose everything. That night, Pahom dreams. A man lying dead on the ground. He looks closer. It's himself. The devil stands over the body, laughing. He wakes up and dismisses it. What a weird dream. At dawn, he starts walking. The land is extraordinary. He keeps pushing the boundary wider, just a little more, just that valley, just that hill, just that meadow. The sun climbs. He's gone too far. He starts running, boots off, coat thrown, lungs burning, heart slamming. The sun touches the horizon. He can see the finish. He sprints. He collapses across the line just as the sun disappears and dies with his hands touching the cap. The chief laughs. What a fine fellow. He gained so much land. His servants pick him up, dig him a grave. Six feet from his head to his heels was all he needed. Crazy, man. I'm just gonna leave it at that.
